Brands mainly have three options: sell it to a liquidator for pennies, pay to store it, or pay to landfill it. Every option loses money. In Canada, 1.1 million tonnes of apparel went to landfill in 2021. Yet 85% of clothing waste can be recycled, reused or resold.

Existing options don't really fix any of it. Liquidators sell unsorted pallets, so buyers are gambling on what's inside. Bundle sellers usually sort by brand, but people typically want a look, not the brand itself. Brands lose profit, and a side effect is the environment. 15 million garments reach Ghana every week, and about 40% of them end up as waste.

Next Rack · Aaspreet, Mona, Hilarie, Suhani
Next Rack is a business that helps Canadian clothing brands deal with excess inventory and customer returns in a more organized and sustainable way. Instead of brands having to sort, store, or liquidate unwanted clothing themselves, Next Rack collects the surplus inventory, inspects it, and sorts it into categories based on style, colour, clothing type, and condition.

Next Rack · Aaspreet, Mona, Hilarie, Suhani
Our implementation begins with a two-month pilot in the Lower Mainland. We will purchase about 250 returned and overstock garments from two or three small Canadian brands, at roughly two dollars per garment. We then inspect each piece by hand. About one in five returns is defective, and those garments go to textile recyclers. The rest are sorted into themes, chosen once we see which styles we have the most of.
